TIME_VALUE_DESIGN(7)        RSpade Framework Manual       TIME_VALUE_DESIGN(7)

NAME
       time_value_design - how to store the dates and times users record:
       instants, calendar dates, and an intended day with an actual time

TAGS
       time, models, schema

SYNOPSIS
       An INSTANT          UTC DATETIME                 created_at, a meeting
       A CALENDAR DATE     DATE                         a due date, a holiday
       AN INTENDED DAY,    DATE (the day)               "3 hours on the proposal,
       OPTIONALLY WITH AN  + optional UTC DATETIMEs      Monday" - maybe "10 PM
       ACTUAL TIME         + the zone it was entered in  to 1 AM"

       Totals, timecards, quotas, per-day views and billing read the DAY.
       A record with a clock time stores the zone that clock was ENTERED in.

       Every RSpade application is recommended to follow these conventions.

DESCRIPTION
       Rsx_Time and Rsx_Date are the primitives: an instant (UTC, converted per
       viewer) and a calendar date (no zone, the same for every viewer). Most
       business applications - project management, work tracking, calendars,
       timecards, billing - also record a COMPOUND value no primitive names on
       its own: the DAY a piece of work belongs to, chosen by the person who
       logged it, and sometimes the actual TIME it happened. This page is the
       framework's guidance for that value, and for the mistakes every such
       application otherwise discovers from a client report that evening work
       "moved to Tuesday".

       What is a framework PRIMITIVE and what is GUIDANCE:

           Primitives   Rsx_Time, Rsx_Date, the display-zone resolution
                        (rsx:man time), and the day-and-clock helpers below.
           Guidance     everything else here: the three kinds, the rules, the
                        column conventions, the dead ends. Nothing enforces it;
                        an application that follows it does not lose data.

THE THREE KINDS OF TIME VALUE
   An Instant
       A moment: a meeting at 1:00 PM, created_at, a timer start. Stored as a
       UTC DATETIME, transported as UTC ISO 8601, shown in each viewer's zone.

   A Calendar Date
       A day that has no zone: a holiday, a due date, an invoice date. Stored as
       DATE and read identically by every viewer (Rsx_Date). Never converted:
       converting a date moves it a day for any evening value west of UTC.

   An Intended Day, Optionally With An Actual Time
       "3 hours on the proposal, Monday" - and perhaps "from 10 PM to 1 AM".
       Stored as a DATE (the day), optional UTC DATETIMEs (the actual time),
       and the IANA zone the time was ENTERED in.

       The DAY is a business fact - which day of the week, which week's
       timecard, which quota day, which invoice period the work belongs to -
       chosen by the person who logged it. The TIME, when given, is a record
       of what actually happened. They are DIFFERENT FACTS and legitimately
       disagree for a reader: 11 PM Central on Monday is 12:00 AM Tuesday in
       Eastern, and the work is still Monday's. That drift is correct and must
       never be "fixed".

RULES
       1.  Totals, timecards, quotas, per-day views and billing read the DAY
           column, never a date derived from the time. Grouping hours by the
           instant's date moves a person's evening work onto the next day for
           every viewer east of them, silently.

       2.  A record with a clock time stores the zone that clock was ENTERED
           in. A UTC instant alone cannot say what wall clock the person typed,
           on which day; without the zone, any surface that shows the DAY beside
           the TIME must guess one, and the viewer's zone is wrong whenever
           viewer and author differ.

       3.  An edit surface reads and re-applies the clock in the RECORDED zone,
           never the editor's. A form that shows the day with the time read in
           the editor's zone, then saves both back in the editor's zone, moves
           the work a day for an editor elsewhere who changed nothing.

       4.  A writer that re-applies the SAME instant keeps the recorded zone. A
           form that seeds the date and the time in the viewer's zone and posts
           them back reproduces the instant; that is not a re-entry, and
           re-labelling the zone would re-derive the day.

       5.  A record whose day and time live on two rows keeps them on one day
           by rule, in the MODEL: a saving hook refuses a move it cannot make
           (with a sentence a person can act on), a saved hook carries the move
           it can.

       6.  A downstream copy of the day is FROZEN. A billing line that copies a
           time entry's day locks the entry from then on, so the copy cannot
           drift; an application that lets the source change after a copy owes
           a sync rule, stated.

       7.  "Today" in background code is somebody's today, named explicitly. A
           task has no viewer: Rsx_Date::today() there is the SITE's day. Code
           about a PERSON resolves that person's zone; code about a RECORD with a
           recorded zone uses that zone.

       8.  Cron is wall-clock in the SERVER zone (rsx:man tasks, SCHEDULES AND
           TIMEZONES). A task whose correctness depends on a person's midnight
           cannot be one daily schedule: run it hourly and judge each record in
           its own zone.

       9.  Never derive a date by slicing a string or by SQL DATE() of an
           instant. substr($time, 0, 10) and DATE(started_at) read the UTC date,
           the next day for every evening record in the Americas. Convert to the
           zone, then take the date: Rsx_Time::local_date($time, $zone).

       10. A list mixing dated and timed rows needs a sort EXPRESSION, not a
           column: a NULL instant on every dated row sorts to one end whatever
           its date. Place a dated row at 01:01 on its own day in the viewer's
           zone, computed at query time and never stored - 01:01, not midnight,
           because a fixed offset applied across a daylight-saving boundary is
           an hour out, and an anchor at midnight is one hour from the previous
           day.

       11. A date window over instants converts each bound PER DATE -
           day_start_utc($from, $zone) inclusive, day_after_utc($to, $zone)
           exclusive - which keeps it right across a daylight-saving change;
           dated rows match by plain date in the same query.

COLUMN CONVENTIONS
       For a record of the third kind:

           work_date      DATE NOT NULL     the intended day
           started_at     DATETIME NULL     the actual start (UTC), if given
           ended_at       DATETIME NULL     the actual end (UTC), if given
           timezone       VARCHAR(64) NULL  the zone started_at/ended_at were
                                            entered in; NULL exactly when no
                                            time was given; validated with
                                            Rsx_Time::is_timezone() on save

       A date is stored as a DATE. A "placeholder time" (00:00 or 06:00 meaning
       "no time") is never a substitute: see DEAD ENDS.

THE DAY-AND-CLOCK HELPERS
       PHP and JavaScript, same names, same answers (rsx:man time):

           Rsx_Time::local_date($time, $zone)      "2026-10-26" - the day an
                                                   instant falls on in $zone
           Rsx_Time::local_clock($time, $zone)     "23:00"
           Rsx_Time::combine_date_and_time($date, $clock, $zone)
                                                   the instant a date + wall
                                                   clock typed in $zone mean;
                                                   null clock = null
           Rsx_Time::day_start_utc($date, $zone)   the instant $date begins
           Rsx_Time::day_after_utc($date, $zone)   the instant the next day
                                                   begins (exclusive bound)

       PRINTING a record of this kind uses the recorded zone too, which names
       itself only when it is not the reader's (rsx:man time, FORMATTING):

           Rsx_Time::format_time_range($entry->started_at, $entry->ended_at, $entry->timezone)
                                                   "Oct 9, 2026 10:00 - 11:30 AM IST"
                                                   for a Chicago reader

       Every one returns ISO strings (a date as "Y-m-d"); a raw query binding
       wraps an instant in Rsx_Time::to_database(). A clock skipped by a
       spring-forward jump resolves to the instant after it, an ambiguous
       fall-back clock to its first occurrence. The JavaScript twins work in any
       named zone, not only the browser's - which is what a calendar drawn in a
       user's chosen zone needs.

WORKED SHAPES
   The Writer, Keeping The Recorded Zone On An Unchanged Instant
           public function set_work_time(string $work_date, ?string $start_clock,
                                         ?string $end_clock = null, ?string $zone = null): void
           {
               $zone = $zone ?? Rsx_Time::get_user_timezone();
               $starts_at = Rsx_Time::combine_date_and_time($work_date, $start_clock, $zone);
               $ends_at = Rsx_Time::combine_date_and_time($work_date, $end_clock, $zone);

               $unchanged = $this->started_at !== null && Rsx_Time::is_timezone($this->timezone)
                   && Rsx_Time::to_iso($this->started_at) === $starts_at
                   && ($this->ended_at ? Rsx_Time::to_iso($this->ended_at) : null) === $ends_at;

               $this->work_date = $work_date;
               $this->started_at = $starts_at;
               $this->ended_at = $ends_at;
               if ($starts_at === null) {
                   $this->timezone = null;
               } elseif (!$unchanged) {
                   $this->timezone = $zone;
               }
           }

   The Edit Surface, Re-Applying In The Recorded Zone
           $zone = $entry->exists && $entry->started_at !== null ? $entry->timezone : null;
           $entry->set_work_time($params['work_date'], $params['start'] ?: null,
                                 $params['end'] ?: null, $zone);

       And the form is SEEDED in that zone too:
           'start' => Rsx_Time::local_clock($entry->started_at, $entry->timezone)

   One Day For A Container And Its Child, In The Model
           static::saving(function (self $item) {   // refuse what cannot move
               if ($item->exists && $item->isDirty('work_date') && $item->child_is_billed()) {
                   throw new \RuntimeException('This work is already billed; it cannot move to another day.');
               }
           });
           static::saved(function (self $item) {    // move what can
               if ($item->wasChanged('work_date')) {
                   $item->sync_child_day();
               }
           });

   A Sweep Judged In Each Record's Own Zone
           #[Task] #[Schedule('hourly')]
           public static function stop_stale_timers(Task_Instance $task, array $params = [])
           {
               foreach ($running as $timer) {
                   $zone = Rsx_Time::is_timezone($timer->timezone) ? $timer->timezone : $site_zone;
                   if ($timer->work_date < Rsx_Time::local_date(Rsx_Time::now(), $zone)) {
                       // the timer's own day is over
                   }
               }
           }

   A Date Window Over Both Shapes
           $query->where(fn ($timed) => $timed->whereNotNull('started_at')
                   ->where('started_at', '<', Rsx_Time::to_database(Rsx_Time::day_after_utc($to, $zone)))
                   ->whereRaw('COALESCE(ended_at, started_at) >= ?',
                       [Rsx_Time::to_database(Rsx_Time::day_start_utc($from, $zone))]))
               ->orWhere(fn ($dated) => $dated->whereNull('started_at')
                   ->whereBetween('work_date', [$from, $to]));

DEAD ENDS
       Each is the obvious first design, and each has damaged real data.

       A floating midnight plus a flag. One DATETIME holds either a real instant
           or "Y-m-d 00:00:00" meaning "this date, no zone", with is_all_day
           saying which. Every reader that ignores the flag shows an imported
           14 September entry as 13 September, 7:00 PM; every reader that takes
           the first ten characters of a real instant shows evening work on the
           next day.

       A placeholder time plus "do not print the clock". Date-only work stored
           as 06:00 local with is_time_unspecified. Right on screen, and still a
           rule every reader must remember - the ones that do not print "6:00 AM"
           for work nobody timed. Store a date as a date: there is nothing to
           forget.

       One stored zone per account, overwritten by "automatic". Two browsers
           fight over it, and a headless sign-in sets a real person's account to
           UTC. (The framework's own display zone no longer works this way:
           rsx:man time, TIMEZONE INITIALIZATION.)

       A zone per session. Mints sessions for visitors who need none, and still
           cannot render the first page right.

       A UTC instant with no recorded zone, read in the viewer's zone beside a
           stored DAY. The day-shift defect of rule 3.

MEASURING
       MySQL's CONVERT_TZ() with a NAMED zone returns NULL on a server whose
       zone tables are not loaded - the default in many images - and a
       comparison against NULL is never true, so an audit query "count rows
       whose local date disagrees with work_date" silently reports zero. Audit
       in PHP (Rsx_Time::local_date()), or with explicit offsets - never with a
       named-zone CONVERT_TZ() you have not checked returns a value.
