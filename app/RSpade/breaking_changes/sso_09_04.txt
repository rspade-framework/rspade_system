FEDERATED SIGN-IN (SSO) - THE ENGINE SHIPS, THE SCREENS DO NOT
Date: 2026-09-04

IF YOU DO NOTHING: nothing breaks and nothing changes - your users see no
    "Continue with Google" button, cannot connect or disconnect a provider
    account anywhere in your application, and the whole subsystem stays
    dormant until you switch a provider on in .env, write one
    sso.identity.unlinked handler, and port the screens described below.

SUMMARY
    RSpade now ships federated sign-in: "Continue with Google / Microsoft /
    Facebook / Apple / X" on a login page, and a connected-accounts list
    behind a settings screen. A sixth provider is one composer package and one
    config block.

    The framework owns the CEREMONY - the state value, PKCE, the token
    exchange, the throttle budget, the failure record, and the normalized
    identity that comes out. Your APPLICATION owns the POLICY - whether an
    unknown Google account may become an account here, which addresses it may
    match, where a signed-in identity lands - and it says so through event
    hooks that all FAIL CLOSED. A framework that guessed at any of those would
    be inventing "create an account", which it must never do for you.

    All of that arrived with the pull. What did NOT arrive is the interface:
    the buttons on your login page, the handler file that answers the policy
    question, and the Connected Accounts section of your settings screen. Those
    live in /rsx/, which is yours.

    Full contract: php artisan rsx:man sso
    Recipes and gotchas: the `rspade:sso` skill

WHAT IS MISSING IN YOUR APPLICATION
    THREE THINGS, and the first one is not optional if you enable a provider
    at all.

    1. THE POLICY HANDLER. Without an #[OnEvent('sso.identity.unlinked')]
       handler, every provider account that is not already connected to a
       local identity is refused with "No account is connected to this
       sign-in." That is the deliberate fail-closed default - it is safe, and
       it means a brand-new provider account can never sign in or sign up.
       Existing users cannot connect one either, because there is nowhere to
       press Connect (item 3).

    2. THE LOGIN BUTTONS. Nobody can start a ceremony from your login page
       until <Sso_Buttons /> is on it. The endpoints exist and answer, but the
       only way to reach them would be to type a /_sso/ URL by hand.

    3. THE CONNECTED ACCOUNTS SECTION. A signed-in user cannot connect a
       provider to the account they already have, cannot see which providers
       are connected, and cannot disconnect one. The operator can do it with
       rsx:users:sso:unlink; the user cannot do any of it.

    Who is blocked: every user of your application, for signing in and for
    self-service; and you, for the one policy decision only your product can
    make.

THE REFERENCE IMPLEMENTATION
    Release-current and readable on your own disk, under
    system/app/RSpade/resource/reference_app/ :

    handlers/Sso_Handlers.php
        THE POLICY, and the file to read first. All five hooks in one class:
        sso.identity.unlinked (the invite-aware verified-email-match mode,
        with the account-takeover reasoning written out in full and the other
        three modes named in a comment block), sso.login.authorize (permits,
        and explains why it must mirror the password door),
        sso.two_factor.verify_url, sso.login.destination and
        sso.link.destination.

    app/login/login_index.blade.php
        The buttons on a login page, and the "or" divider. Both halves sit
        inside one @if (Rsx_Sso::is_enabled()) - the component renders nothing
        on its own when no provider is live, so a page that wants a rule above
        it has to ask the same question.

    app/login/login_controller.php
        post_login_destination() made public, so ONE function computes the
        landing page for the password path and the SSO hook alike. A
        destination computed twice drifts.

    app/login/accept_invite/accept_invite_controller.php
        The finish-registration pattern, worked: __matching_pending_identity()
        detects a parked identity whose asserted address matches the
        invitation, the password becomes optional server-side in that branch,
        and Rsx_Sso::link_pending() is called INSIDE the transaction that
        creates the account - so a refused link rolls the account back rather
        than leaving one nobody can sign in to.

    app/login/accept_invite/create_account.blade.php
        The invitee-facing half: the "You're signing up with {provider}"
        notice and the password field marked optional.

    app/frontend/settings/password_security/settings_password_security_action.jqhtml
    app/frontend/settings/password_security/settings_password_security_action.js
    app/frontend/settings/password_security/settings_password_security_action.scss
        The Connected Accounts section: one row per LIVE provider with a
        Connect or Disconnect button, PLUS a disconnect-only row for a
        connection whose provider has since been switched off. The whole
        section is absent - not empty - when Rsx_Sso.is_enabled() is false.
        The SCSS rule sizes the inline brand marks.

PORTING IT
    THE HANDLER IS THE PART THAT IS ACTUALLY A DECISION. The screens are
    ordinary UI work; the policy is product design, and copying the template's
    without reading it is the one mistake that has a security consequence.

    1. PICK YOUR POLICY MODE. The man page (php artisan rsx:man sso, THE FOUR
       POLICY MODES) carries all four with code:

         VERIFIED-EMAIL MATCH   connect a provider identity to an account it
                                can PROVE belongs to the same person. What the
                                template ships, invite-aware.
         AUTO-PROVISION         any verified provider identity gets an
                                account. Open signup through SSO.
         INVITE-ONLY, STRICT    nobody signs in until an administrator invited
                                the address.
         FINISH REGISTRATION    send the browser to a page of your own that
                                reads Rsx_Sso::pending(), collects what your
                                product needs, and calls link_pending() on
                                submit.

       *** NEVER MATCH AN UNVERIFIED EMAIL. *** A provider that lets a user
       type any address into a profile hands over a CLAIM, not a fact.
       Matching a claim against your login_users means anybody who can name
       your user's address at such a provider signs in as them - no password,
       no notification. email_verified is the provider's own assertion and it
       is the only thing that makes a match safe. Google and Apple assert it;
       Microsoft, Facebook and X do not, so those three will never satisfy a
       verified-match policy. Write the null-email case too: X routinely sends
       none, and Facebook's may be declined by the user.

    2. WRITE rsx/handlers/Sso_Handlers.php (any class name; the attributes are
       what matter). At minimum sso.identity.unlinked. Add
       sso.two_factor.verify_url if any identity in your install can hold a
       second factor - it is a shouldnt_happen() without one, because the
       framework refuses to begin a challenge it cannot tell the user how to
       answer. Add sso.login.destination pointing at whatever function your
       password login already returns, and sso.login.authorize enforcing
       exactly what your password login enforces - no more, and above all no
       less.

    3. PUT THE BUTTONS ON YOUR LOGIN PAGE, wherever your design puts a
       secondary sign-in path, guarded by Rsx_Sso::is_enabled() together with
       whatever divider or heading you want above them.

    4. ADD A CONNECTED ACCOUNTS SECTION wherever your application keeps
       security settings. It needs no new endpoint: identities_list(),
       link_begin() and identity_unlink() are framework endpoints already
       gated #[Auth('is_logged_in')]. Two details are not optional - hide the
       section entirely when nothing is enabled (window.rsxapp.sso is absent,
       so a heading would announce a feature that does not exist), and render
       a disconnect row for a connection whose provider has since been
       switched off, or the user cannot remove something that is really there.

    5. IF YOUR SIGN-UP OR INVITE FLOW SHOULD ACCEPT A PROVIDER IDENTITY, teach
       it the pending identity: read Rsx_Sso::pending(), match the address if
       your flow has one to match, make the password optional in that branch,
       and call Rsx_Sso::link_pending() inside the same transaction that
       creates the account.

    YOUR NAVIGATION, YOUR PERMISSION MODEL AND YOUR DESIGN SYSTEM DECIDE THE
    FINAL PLACEMENT. This is a map, not a diff to paste blindly - the
    reference app's settings layout and login page are its own, and the
    framework surfaces they drive are the only part that is fixed.

VERIFICATION
    1. Configure one provider in .env (Google is the least ceremonial), with
       https://<your host>/_sso/google/callback registered as the redirect URI
       in the provider's console.
    2. Load your login page: the buttons and your divider appear. Set
       SSO_GOOGLE_ENABLED=false and reload: both are gone, with no empty
       chrome left behind.
    3. Press Continue with Google as a user whose account matches your policy:
       you land where sso.login.destination says, and
       `php artisan rsx:users:sso:dump --user=<that identity>` shows the
       connection.
    4. Press it as an identity your policy declines: you land back on the
       login page with your policy's message, or the framework's "No account
       is connected to this sign-in.", and nothing is signed in.
    5. With a second factor enrolled on the identity, sign in through the
       provider: you land on your 2FA verify page, NOT signed in, and the
       challenge completes the login.
    6. On your settings screen: Connect adds a row, Disconnect removes it, and
       the list repaints from the endpoint's own answer.
    7. Sign in twice through the same provider account: the second time is a
       straight sign-in with no application involvement at all.
